# 2025 back-test against RAIF (Andalucía)

Generated by `scripts/backtest_raif.py` from `data/processed/features/` (ERA5-Land, olive-area weighted province means) and `data/processed/ground_truth/raif_olive_labels.csv`. Raw numbers: `data/processed/backtest/results.json`. No threshold was tuned. This only measures how well the current proxies and rules agree with what RAIF reported.

**Scope:** one year, 250 cleaned labels (2025, Andalusian provinces, good pairing, usable date). Dates are coarse (month or part-of-month, ±5–15 days). Only Andalucía has ground truth; the other four regions are not scored.

## Results

## What the results mean

- **Phenology:** the late bias comes from the assumption that dormancy release happens on 1 January. The real release date depends on chilling, and the De Melo-Abreu chill-unit model is not implemented. The 2025 ERA5 file also starts on 1 January, so earlier degree-days are missing. The test can't separate those two causes. Only three provinces have F-stage reports, so even a pass would prove little.
- **Peacock spot:** the correlation is real in this sample, but it is flat across lags and the data are seasonal. Wet spring and autumn weeks coincide with the weeks RAIF samples, so part of the signal may be season, not infection. A fair test needs several years and a within-season comparison.
- **Olive fly:** with 14 points there is no usable result. Fly pressure also depends on fruit availability and trap management, which weather alone doesn't capture.

## Not testable

- Wind-gust, hail and frost rules have no RAIF labels.
- Castilla-La Mancha, Extremadura, Cataluña and Valencia have no ground truth here.
- Rules that need field observations (41 of 142 rows) cannot be scored from weather data.

## Next steps this suggests

1. Add the Oct–Dec 2024 ERA5 data and implement the chill-unit model, then re-run the phenology test.
2. Extend to 2022–2024 (RAIF has reports for those years) before drawing any conclusion on peacock spot.
3. Keep every rule `validated=false` until an agronomist reviews it.
